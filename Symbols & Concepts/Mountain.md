# Mountain

## Working definition (editorial scaffolding)

A height that is climbed, or that stands at the centre of the world. Gaskell reads it inwardly and in two directions: aspiration, and self-exaltation. Hall and Blavatsky add the world-mountain of the mythologies, which Hall turns into the human head and Blavatsky into an allegory of cosmogony. In Bullinger mountains are only the literal hills of the prophecies he quotes.

## Gaskell (OCR; unreviewed)

There is no entry headed simply MOUNTAIN, and none labelled "higher aspect". The general sense sits under MOUNTAIN OR MOUNT; the only qualified entry is the lower aspect.

- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MOUNTAIN (LOWER ASPECT)|MOUNTAIN (LOWER ASPECT)]] ([[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_522|page_522]]–[[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_523|page_523]]). The definition, OCR exact: "A symbol of self-exalted qualities of the lower nature, such as pride, vanity, ambition, greed, desire for power over others, which qualities must be transmuted into aspiration, zeal, public spirit, etc," — the OCR runs straight on into the temptation text, printed as `Mar. iv. 8, 9` with `tall down` for "fall down". The body places the "exceeding high mountain" in the mind itself.
- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MOUNTAIN OR MOUNT|MOUNTAIN OR MOUNT]] ([[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_522|page_522]]). OCR exact: "A symbol of aspiration towards ideals, or the rise of the soul to higher planes of consciousness." The body reads the Sermon on the Mount (OCR `Mart. v. 1, and viii. 1`): going up is the ascent of the Divine nature in the soul, the disciples are disciplined qualities. It also cites Psalm 48:1.
- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MOUNTAIN TOP|MOUNTAIN TOP]]. OCR exact: "A symbol of the higher planes— atma and buddhi."
- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MOUNTAINS|MOUNTAINS]] repeats the aspiration sense for the plural.

## King James Bible

- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/40 Matthew/Matthew 004#^kjv-mat-4-8|Matthew 4:8]] — the "exceeding high mountain" of the temptation; the text of Gaskell's lower aspect, and cited by Blavatsky.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/40 Matthew/Matthew 005#^kjv-mat-5-1|Matthew 5:1]] and [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/40 Matthew/Matthew 008#^kjv-mat-8-1|8:1]] — going up and coming down the mountain; the text of Gaskell's MOUNTAIN OR MOUNT.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/19 Psalms/Psalms 048#^kjv-psa-48-1|Psalms 48:1]] — "the mountain of his holiness"; bridged from MOUNTAIN OR MOUNT.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/26 Ezekiel/Ezekiel 034#^kjv-eze-34-13|Ezekiel 34:13]]–[[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/26 Ezekiel/Ezekiel 034#^kjv-eze-34-14|34:14]] — the flock fed on "the mountains of Israel"; quoted by Bullinger.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/34 Nahum/Nahum 001#^kjv-nah-1-5|Nahum 1:5]] — "The mountains quake at him"; quoted by Bullinger.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/66 Revelation/Revelation 021#^kjv-rev-21-10|Revelation 21:10]] — John carried to "a great and high mountain" to see the holy city; cited by Bullinger.

## Hall — *The Secret Teachings of All Ages*

- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Atlantis and the Godsof Antiquity#^hall-stota-pdf-85|Atlantis and the Gods of Antiquity, PDF 85]] — the lofty mountain at the centre of Atlantis is "the axle mountain of the world", symbolic of the human head rising out of the four elements of the body; Hall says it gave rise to Olympus, Meru and Asgard.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Ancient Mysteries and Secret Societies Part Three#^hall-stota-pdf-73|Ancient Mysteries Part Three, PDF 73]] — against Hades as darkness below, the gods "inhabit the tops of mountains"; the Eleusinian neophyte passes through ever brighter chambers to portray the ascent.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Rosicrucian Doctrines and Tenets#^hall-stota-pdf-448|Rosicrucian Doctrines and Tenets, PDF 448]] — reporting an unnamed manuscript: the path of attainment has three steps "which he calls mountains", Mount Sophia, Mount Qabbalah and Mount Magia.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Theory and Practice of Alchemy Part Two#^hall-stota-pdf-501|Alchemy Part Two, PDF 501]] — a quoted Rosicrucian letter: a mountain "in the midst of the earth", invisible, full of treasure and ringed by beasts, to be found by each man's own labour. A caption on [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Theory and Practice of Alchemy Part Two#^hall-stota-pdf-504|PDF 504]] gives Eugenius Philalethes' "Mountains of the Moon".

Hall does not cite Gaskell.

## Bullinger — *The Witness of the Stars*

Bullinger gives the mountain no meaning of its own; no constellation is one. The word arrives only inside prophecies he quotes for other purposes.

- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/17 Chapter III The Sign CANCER#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00049|Cancer (Ursa Major)]] — Ezekiel 34:12–16 quoted for the gathering of the scattered flock.
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/15 Chapter I The Sign TAURUS#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00041|Taurus (Eridanus)]] — Nahum 1:5–6 quoted for the fire of judgment.

## Blavatsky — *Isis Unveiled*

- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 1 Science/Text/10 Chapter V The Ether or Astral Light#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_156|Vol. 1, page 156]]–[[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 1 Science/Text/10 Chapter V The Ether or Astral Light#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_157|157]] — her own voice. Mithras is the son of Bordj, "the Persian mundane mountain"; Siva is personated by Meru. All such world-mountains hold "the allegorically-expressed description of primal cosmogony", and the mountain's interior is "the matrix of the universe".
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/16 Chapter X The Devil-Myth#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_513|Vol. 2, page 513]] — her own comparison, citing Matthew 4:8–9: Jesus tempted on the mountain is set beside Buddha tempted by Mara. Her point is the parallel of the two temptations; she does not interpret the mountain.

## Cross-source comparison (editorial)

| Relation | Supported observation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Ascent | Gaskell's MOUNTAIN OR MOUNT, Hall's three Rosicrucian mountains and his Eleusinian ascent all make climbing a figure for spiritual growth | Hall is reporting a manuscript and a ritual, not defining a symbol. He does not cite Gaskell |
| World-mountain | Hall (PDF 85) and Blavatsky (pages 156–157) both gather Meru and similar peaks into one type | Hall resolves it into the human head, Blavatsky into cosmogony. Gaskell's ALBORDJ and OLYMPUS entries were not read for this hub |
| The temptation mountain | Gaskell and Blavatsky both use Matthew 4:8–9 | Gaskell reads an inner state of pride; Blavatsky draws a historical parallel with Buddhism |
| Two directions in Gaskell | The same height is aspiration in one entry and self-exaltation in another | Gaskell's own split. Do not average it into "mountain = higher consciousness" |
| Bullinger against the rest | His mountains are places in prophecy | Absence of a symbolic reading is not a rejection of one |
