# Creation

## Working definition (editorial scaffolding)

The bringing of a world into being. The sources in this vault split on what kind of event that is. Gaskell, Hall's Qabbalistic chapters and Blavatsky describe an emanation or unfolding from an unmanifest source, usually in graded stages and, for Blavatsky, repeated without beginning or end. Bullinger keeps the biblical sense: one creation by a personal Creator, which shows his power, now "groans" under a curse and waits for Christ's return. Keep those two frames apart.

## Gaskell (OCR; unreviewed)

Primary entry: [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/C/CREATION OR EMANATION OF THE SPIRITUAL AND MATERIAL UNIVERSES|CREATION OR EMANATION OF THE SPIRITUAL AND MATERIAL UNIVERSES]] (`page_190`–`page_191`).

Machine-selected short definition (OCR exact):

> The potential and unmanifest, becoming the actual and manifest in a scheme of being in which the spiritual and mental precede the astral and physical.

The body is almost all quotation. It cites Erigena (through Alice Gardner) on creation as the thinking of a thought, the Zohar (through Isaac Myer) on a perfect invisible universe above as the model of the visible one below, Hermes, an Upanishad, the Book of the Dead, a Tuscan scheme of twelve thousand years, and a sermon by R. J. Campbell arguing that creation "has never had a beginning". The OCR is rough in places (`ulsu`, `reat`, `wo are obliged`). No Bible reference in this entry has been parsed into a bridge note.

## King James Bible

- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/01 Genesis/Genesis 001#^kjv-gen-1-1|Genesis 1:1]] — the opening act; Gaskell cites it under HEAVEN AND EARTH, not under this entry.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/01 Genesis/Genesis 001#^kjv-gen-1-2|Genesis 1:2]] — the formless earth and the Spirit on the waters; quoted by Gaskell and alluded to by Blavatsky.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/01 Genesis/Genesis 001#^kjv-gen-1-14|Genesis 1:14]] — lights "for signs, and for seasons"; Bullinger's key text.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/01 Genesis/Genesis 002#^kjv-gen-2-2|Genesis 2:2]] — the seventh-day rest, which Blavatsky calls a "cloak" (she prints no verse number there).
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/45 Romans/Romans 001#^kjv-rom-1-20|Romans 1:20]] — the invisible things seen "from the creation of the world"; quoted in Bullinger's Introduction and cited by Gaskell under MONAD OF LIFE.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/45 Romans/Romans 008#^kjv-rom-8-22|Romans 8:22]] — "the whole creation groaneth"; cited by Gaskell under ADOPTION. Bullinger's repeated "groaning creation" echoes it, without a printed reference in the passages read.

## Hall — *The Secret Teachings of All Ages*

- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Fundamentals of Qabbalistic Cosmogony#^hall-stota-pdf-361|PDF 361]] — AIN SOPH is the unconditioned ground out of which creations are projected and into which they dissolve; Hall likens it to dark loam feeding many plants.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Fundamentals of Qabbalistic Cosmogony#^hall-stota-pdf-364|PDF 364]] — the "40 spheres of creation" are arranged as four worlds: Atziluth, Briah, Yetzirah, Assiah.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Tree of the Sephiroth#^hall-stota-pdf-377|PDF 377]] — the order of the Creation is a "Divine Lightning Flash" running down through the four worlds.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Qabbalistic Keys to the Creation of Man#^hall-stota-pdf-390|PDF 390]] — the Elohim pattern the "inferior creation" on their own shadows seen in the Abyss; Hall presents this as a doctrine of the Jewish Mysteries.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Fifteen Rosicrucian and Qabbalistic Diagrams#^hall-stota-pdf-455|PDF 455]] — a diagram of the seven days of creation in three worlds, lettered with words from Genesis 1. Hall is describing the plate.

## Bullinger — *The Witness of the Stars*

- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/03 Introduction#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00002|Introduction]] — since the creation of the world God has had a witness; Romans 1 and Psalm 19 show that the witness is the heavens.
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/03 Introduction#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00004|Introduction, later section]] — he quotes a Babylonian "Creation Tablet" in which the Creator fixes the twelve mansions and star-groups, as evidence for the age of the signs.
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/02 Preface#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00001|Preface]] — apart from Christ's return there is "no hope for a groaning creation"; the fixed point is Genesis 3:15.

## Blavatsky — *Isis Unveiled*

- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/10 Chapter VI Esoteric Doctrines of Buddhism Parodied in Christianity#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_265|vol. 2, p. 265]] — in her own voice, expounding what she calls the esoteric doctrine: an outbreathing of the unknown essence produces the world and an inhalation undoes it, in an infinite series. She calls it "puerile" to name these creative principles God.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/09 Chapter V Mysteries of the Kabala#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_220|vol. 2, p. 220]] — reporting the kabalists with approval: they never held creation *ex nihilo*, but many earlier worlds made and destroyed, each thing evolving from a more spiritual prototype.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/14 Chapter IX The Vedas and the Bible#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_418|vol. 2, p. 418]] — her own claim: the Lord's rest after creation was an allegory used as a screen for another meaning.

## Cross-source comparison (editorial)

| Relation | Supported observation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Gaskell and Hall | Both describe creation as emanation through ordered levels; Gaskell quotes the Zohar, Hall expounds Qabbalistic worlds | Hall does not cite Gaskell. Gaskell's spiritual, mental, astral and physical planes are not Hall's four worlds; matching them is editorial |
| Gaskell and Blavatsky | Gaskell quotes Campbell against a creation with a beginning; Blavatsky teaches endless outbreathing and denies *ex nihilo* | No mutual citation found. Gaskell's entry is a chain of quotations in unreviewed OCR, so his own position is less certain than hers |
| Bullinger against the rest | He treats creation as a past act of God whose heavens carry a message and whose earth awaits restoration | A contrast, not a variant. Do not turn his "signs" into cycles of emanation |
| Genesis 1 as common text | All four work from Genesis 1 | Same chapter, different questions: plane-structure, Sephirothic order, concealed cosmogony, prophetic sign |
