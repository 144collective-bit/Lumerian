# Incarnation

## Working definition (editorial scaffolding)

The taking of a body by something spiritual. The sources split on what is embodied and how often: Gaskell makes it a universal process (Spirit in Matter, and spiritual egos in human forms), Bullinger means the single first coming of Christ, Blavatsky argues for periodical incarnations and against a unique one, and Hall mostly uses the word for avatars and for the sun or a god entering an animal form. The King James Bible does not use the word.

## Gaskell (OCR; unreviewed)

There is no plain INCARNATION headword in the import. The term is carried by two entries:

- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/I/INCARNATION OF SPIRIT|INCARNATION OF SPIRIT]] (`gaskell-1960-entry-incarnation-of-spirit`; [[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_394|page_394]]–[[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_396|page_396]]). Short definition (OCR exact):

> A symbol of the embodiment of Spirit in Matter, or of the Soul or Self in the lower nature (flesh).

- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/I/INCARNATION OF SOULS|INCARNATION OF SOULS]] (`gaskell-1960-entry-incarnation-of-souls`; [[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_396|page_396]]). Short definition (OCR exact):

> The descent of the spiritual egos into human forms on the lower planes.

The first entry places the term between two others: “Incarnation completes involution, and is the point where evolution commences.” It calls incarnation the completion of the Archetypal Man (Christ), then quotes Strauss, Pfleiderer, Phillips Brooks, R. J. Campbell and Underhill on incarnation as an eternal or continuing process. The second entry says the Divine Monad divides into “an enormous but Hmited number” of monads (OCR typo for “limited”) that incarnate in humanity once mind is attained.

A third headword, [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/I/INCARNATION, VAIRAUMATI, HEAVENLY SPRINGTIME|INCARNATION, VAIRAUMATI, HEAVENLY SPRINGTIME]], is an import artefact: three headwords run together on `page_354`. Its body concerns the “Heavenly Springtime” of the Book of the Dead, not incarnation.

Especially relevant neighbouring entries: [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/I/INVOLUTION TION|INVOLUTION TION]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/E/EVOLUTION AND INVOLUTION|EVOLUTION AND INVOLUTION]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/A/ARCHETYPAL MAN|ARCHETYPAL MAN]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/B/BIRTH OF CHRIST|BIRTH OF CHRIST]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/I/IMAGE OF GOD|IMAGE OF GOD]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/B/BLOOD OF THE LAMB|BLOOD OF THE LAMB]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/F/FALL OF MAN|FALL OF MAN]].

## King James Bible

The word “incarnation” does not occur in the King James Bible. The two Gaskell entries print no chapter-and-verse locator, so no verses are listed here.

## Hall — *The Secret Teachings of All Ages*

- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Fishes, Insects, Animals, Reptiles and Birds Part Two#^hall-stota-pdf-277|PDF 277]] — the sign rising at the vernal equinox is “the starry body for the annual incarnation of the sun”; the Apis bull signifies “that the God-Mind is incarnated in the body of a beast,” and man's lower personality is the Apis in which Osiris incarnates.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Fishes, Insects, Animals, Reptiles and Birds Part One#^hall-stota-pdf-255|PDF 255]] — Vishnu, “who takes upon himself ten forms for the redemption of the universe,” came from the mouth of a fish in his first incarnation.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Human Body in Symbolism#^hall-stota-pdf-227|PDF 227]] — “According to the Mystery teachings, not all the spiritual nature of man incarnates in matter”; only the lower point of a triangle descends. (The verb, not the noun.)

Hall does not cite Gaskell.

## Bullinger — *The Witness of the Stars*

One use, in the ordinary Christian sense. Under Virgo he distinguishes two prophecies of the woman's seed, one “connected with the first coming in incarnation,” for which he cites Isaiah 7:14 as quoted in Matthew 1:23, and one with the second coming ([[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/05 Chapter I The Sign VIRGO#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00006|Virgo]]).

## Blavatsky — *Isis Unveiled*

- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/18 Chapter XI Comparative Results of Buddhism and Christianity#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_535|Vol. 2, page 535]] — she reports the Brahman and Buddhist position: they would not deny the Christian incarnation, because “The very corner-stone of their religious system is periodical incarnations of the Deity.” On [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/18 Chapter XI Comparative Results of Buddhism and Christianity#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_536|page 536]] she adds in her own voice that it is hard “to avoid sharing this doctrine.”
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/07 Chapter III Divisions Amongst the Early Christians#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_153|Vol. 2, page 153]] — her own account of the kabalists: when the immortal spirit is fully united with a living man, “A *god* was incarnate. But how rare such incarnations!” She insists the ancients never took such men for incarnations of the one supreme God.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/11 Chapter VII Early Christian Heresies and Secret Societies#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_337|Vol. 2, page 337]] — polemic in her own voice against a “*first and only* incarnation” that brought nothing to mark it off from earlier virgin-born saviours.

## Cross-source comparison (editorial)

| Relation | Supported observation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Process versus event | Gaskell and the authors he quotes treat incarnation as continuous; Bullinger means one first coming | Gaskell does not deny the Gospel event in these entries; he reads it as a symbol |
| Editorial resemblance, Blavatsky | Both reject a once-only reading in favour of repeated or universal embodiment | Blavatsky speaks of rare perfected men and periodic saviours; Gaskell of every soul. No citation of *Isis Unveiled* appears in these two entries |
| Editorial resemblance, Hall | Hall's Apis passage has a god incarnate in the lower personality, close to Gaskell's “Self in the lower nature” | Hall does not cite Gaskell, and most of his uses concern Vishnu's avatars or the sun |
