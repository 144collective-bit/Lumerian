# Death, Resurrection, and Initiation

## Working definition (editorial scaffolding)

Three neighbouring vocabularies: the death and rising narrated in the installed Pauline text; Gaskell’s reading of resurrection as the Higher Self becoming actual, and of initiation as lower and higher lines; Hall’s dramatic “second death” and interior “resurrection” of the Christos. They share words; they do not share one doctrine.

## From E. W. Bullinger, The Witness of the Stars (6 November 2026)

Capricornus (the dying goat) is completed by Delphinus (the fish leaping upward, “the dead One rising again”), and Aries then shows a living lamb after the slain goat. Lupus is the victim who lays down his own life. Sources: [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/10 Chapter I The Sign CAPRICORNUS#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00022|CAPRICORNUS]], [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/10 Chapter I The Sign CAPRICORNUS#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00025|Delphinus]].

- Tier: source-specific interpretation. Contrast with Hall’s interior initiatory death already labelled separately on this hub.
- Limit: He is narrating Christ’s death and resurrection for a people, not an initiation drama.
- See [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Evidence/Crossrefs/Supported Connections|supported connections]].

He pairs Capricornus with the two goats of [[Leviticus 016#^kjv-lev-16-22|Leviticus 16]] and with [[John 012#^kjv-jhn-12-24|John 12:24]]. Footnote 69 records the Egyptian scarabaeus (the Denderah figure he prefers to the crab in Cancer) as an emblem of the resurrection of the body. That emblem is a cited tradition, not an independent Egyptological finding.

## From H. P. Blavatsky, Isis Unveiled (6 November 2026)

In "Before the Veil" she reports Theon's five parts of Platonic / Eleusinian initiation: purification, admission, *epopteia*, investiture, and communion with God ([[05 Before the Veil#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_xiv|pages xiv]]–[[05 Before the Veil#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_xv|xv]]). The glossary "Mysteries" treats the rites as *teletai* / "finishings" analogous to death, promising fuller treatment in later (still unread) chapters.

- Tier: source-specific interpretation. Contrast of frames with Bullinger's historical-redemptive death and resurrection pictured in Capricornus / Delphinus (already on this hub) and with Hall's later initiatory survey — differing frames, not treated here as directly contradictory propositions about one named object.
- Limit: The glossary itself defers detail to subsequent chapters.
- See [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Evidence/Crossrefs/Supported Connections|supported connections]].

## From H. P. Blavatsky, Isis Unveiled — Volume 1, Chapter II (6 November 2026)

She quotes Henry More on apparitions in Samuel's shape: many necromantic shades are "ludicrous spirits, not the souls of the deceased," yet More is "clear for the appearing of the soul of Samuel" and also allows personation ([[07 Chapter II Phenomena and Forces#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_54|pages 54-55]]). The installed narrative of that episode is [[1 Samuel 028#^kjv-1sa-28-13|1 Samuel 28]]; she prints no chapter number. Later she describes a genuine *umbra* as luminous, vapory, and impalpable, against cabinet materializations (page 69).

- Tier: source-specific interpretation; editorial attachment of 1 Samuel 28. Contrast of question with Bullinger's historical-redemptive death already on this hub.
- Limit: More's two possibilities are not reduced to one vault doctrine.
- See [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Notes/Chapter II Phenomena and Forces - Analytical Note|Chapter II analytical note]].

## From H. P. Blavatsky, Isis Unveiled — Volume 1, Chapter IV (6 November 2026)

Defending immortality against scientific atheism, she pairs a botanist's remark that a seed cannot develop unless partly consumed with St. Paul: “it is not quickened unless it die” ([[09 Chapter IV Theories Respecting Psychic Phenomena#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_114|page 114]]), matching [[1 Corinthians 015#^kjv-1co-15-36|1 Corinthians 15:36]]. Socrates and Cebes (Phaedo) are cited to the same effect: what is produced from death is life.

- Tier: `explicit citation` of the Pauline sentence (verse number editorial); Platonic attachment is hers via the Phaedo footnotes.
- Limit: Contrast of question with Bullinger's historical-redemptive death already on this hub, and with Chapter II's *umbra* discrimination — she is arguing survival, not classifying apparitions here.
- See [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Notes/Chapter IV Theories Respecting Psychic Phenomena - Analytical Note|Chapter IV analytical note]].
