# Man

## Working definition (editorial scaffolding)

"Man" is used four ways in this vault and they do not reduce to one. Gaskell makes man a faculty: mind, higher or lower. Hall makes man the microcosm, the body and constitution in which the universe is copied. Bullinger's "man" is a person, the first man who lost dominion and the Second Man who wins it back. Blavatsky's man is a triune being descending into matter and returning. The main split is between man as one part of the soul (Gaskell) and man as the whole being (the other three).

## Gaskell (OCR; unreviewed)

Primary entry: [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MAN, OR MEN|MAN, OR MEN]] ([[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_477|page_477]]). Short definition, OCR exact:

> A universal symbol of mind in two aspects, higher mind or lower.

The body adds "intellect, reason, intelligence, or mental faculties and qualities", and reads the elder brother of the prodigal-son parable as the intellect that argues and cannot expand of itself. It quotes Philo (OCR typo `whs` for "who"), Swedenborg and Ambrose in support.

The qualified entries all build on that equation of man with mind:

- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MAN AND WIFE|MAN AND WIFE]] — "the mental and emotional natures united in the soul".
- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MAN WITHOUT WOMAN|MAN WITHOUT WOMAN]] — "mind subject to desire and uninfluenced by emotion (woman)".
- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MAN, BAD, OR WICKED|MAN, BAD, OR WICKED]] and [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MAN, BLIND|MAN, BLIND]] — mind bound by lower desire; mind that "reveres the form of religion without knowledge of its substance".
- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MAN MAN, RIGHTEOUS|MAN MAN, RIGHTEOUS]] (headword doubled by OCR) — "the exalted mind about to be united to the Higher Self".
- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/M/MAN BORN AS AN ANIMAL|MAN BORN AS AN ANIMAL]] — an "embryo ego" passing through animal forms (OCR `oxget` for "order"). This note also swallows a separate short entry, `MAN, NATURAL`, defined as "the lower mind united with the desires and affections of the lower nature".

Neighbouring entries: [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/A/ADAM (LOWER ASPECT)|ADAM (LOWER ASPECT)]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/A/ARCHETYPAL MAN|ARCHETYPAL MAN]], [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/S/SON OF MAN|SON OF MAN]] ("son of mind"), [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/F/FALL OF MAN|FALL OF MAN]].

## Hall — *The Secret Teachings of All Ages*

- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Human Body in Symbolism#^hall-stota-pdf-216|PDF 216]] — the human body is "the oldest, the most profound, the most universal of all symbols"; the Mysteries taught that everything outside man has its analogue within him.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Human Body in Symbolism#^hall-stota-pdf-218|PDF 218]] — man's spirit stands to his body as God stands to the objective universe.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Life and Teachings of Thoth Hermes Trismegistus#^hall-stota-pdf-102|PDF 102]] — retelling the Hermetic *Vision*, Hall has the Supreme Mind fashion "a glorious Universal Man", heavenly and not earthy, who then descends toward Nature.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Qabbalistic Keys to the Creation of Man#^hall-stota-pdf-389|PDF 389]] — Adam stands for "the full spiritual nature of man", androgynous; Eve is the mortal portion taken out of it.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Fundamentals of Qabbalistic Cosmogony#^hall-stota-pdf-370|PDF 370]] — the "Grand Man of the Zohar", a universal figure stretched across the four Qabbalistic worlds.

## Bullinger — *The Witness of the Stars*

- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/07 Chapter III The Sign SCORPIO#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00015|Serpens and Ophiuchus]] — the man gripping the serpent: the Devil "robbed the first man of his crown" but cannot take it from "the Second Man".
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/15 Chapter I The Sign TAURUS#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00039|Taurus]] and [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/15 Chapter I The Sign TAURUS#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00040|Orion]] — the coming Judge is "no mere Bull" but "a glorious man, even 'the Son of Man.'"
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/19 Summary#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00055|Summary]] — "Man has ever sought to rob Christ of His glory": man as fallen humanity.

## Blavatsky — *Isis Unveiled*

- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 1 Science/Text/14 Chapter IX Cyclic Phenomena#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_327|Vol. 1, page 327]] — reporting "the ancients", with approval: man is "a trinity of body, astral spirit, and immortal soul", the animal only a duality.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/19 Chapter XII Conclusions and Illustrations#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_588|Vol. 2, page 588]] — her own recapitulation: "Man is also triune", and the astral body or soul is "the real man", who becomes immortal by merging with the spirit.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/10 Chapter VI Esoteric Doctrines of Buddhism Parodied in Christianity#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_277|Vol. 2, page 277]] — her own voice, citing the Sohar: the fall of Adam is "not the personal transgression of man, but simply the law of the dual evolution".
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 1 Science/Text/14 Chapter IX Cyclic Phenomena#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_297|Vol. 1, page 297]] — her exposition of the kabalistic Adam as androgyne, from whom woman is later separated.

## Cross-source comparison (editorial)

| Relation | Supported observation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Gaskell against the rest | Only Gaskell makes "man" a single faculty (mind) paired with woman as emotion | Do not read Hall's or Blavatsky's "man" as "mind"; theirs is the whole constitution |
| Hall and Blavatsky | Both describe an androgynous first Adam and a threefold human make-up | Hall speaks of a "triune nature" (PDF 216) and of a twofold constitution with an intermediary sphere (PDF 218); Blavatsky's three are body, astral soul and spirit. Hall does not cite Gaskell |
| Gaskell and Hall on Genesis | Both treat the Eden story as inward allegory | Gaskell's Adam is fallen lower mind; Hall's Adam is the full spiritual nature. Opposite valuations |
| Bullinger against the three | Bullinger's Man is a historical and coming person, set against a real enemy | His "Second Man" is not Gaskell's "higher mind" nor Hall's Universal Man; the likeness is verbal |
