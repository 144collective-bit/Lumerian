# Self

## Working definition (editorial scaffolding)

"Self" with a capital is the working term of Gaskell's whole dictionary: the Higher Self is the divine life in the soul, and most other symbols are defined by reference to it. Hall and Blavatsky use the same capitalised word, Hall for the immortal part an initiate must find, Blavatsky for the Âtman of Indian philosophy. The King James Bible has almost no such vocabulary, and Bullinger does not use the word at all. The tension is therefore between sources for whom salvation is union with one's own Self and sources for whom the word is only a reflexive pronoun.

## Gaskell (OCR; unreviewed)

Primary entry: [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/S/SELF, LOWER|SELF, LOWER]] ([[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_676|page_676]]–[[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_677|page_677]]). There is no plain `SELF` headword in the import. Short definition, OCR exact:

> This symbol can be taken in two senses,—higher and lower.

The body explains both. In the higher sense it is "the Higher Self incarnate in the human soul and striving to gain the mastery of the lower nature". In the lower sense it is "the illusory and fleeting lower personality". Gaskell's proof text is not biblical: Bhagavad-Gita VI. 5–6, "raise the self by the Self" (the OCR has `bo`, `Tho` and `ia` for "be", "The" and "is"). He glosses it as the ego raising the personality by the Higher Self. The rest is quotation from sermons (Brooks, Wilberforce) and from a *Phil. of Religion* whose author the OCR gives as `Lapp`.

- [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/A/AND LIVING, OR INDIVIDUAL SELF|AND LIVING, OR INDIVIDUAL SELF]] (headword cut short by OCR) — the Logos and the indwelling Spirit: "The Two are One."

## King James Bible

The installed KJV uses "self" only as a reflexive ("mine own self", "selfsame"). No bridge note attaches a verse to SELF, LOWER. The verses below are the nearest attested contacts.

- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/43 John/John 005#^kjv-jhn-5-30|John 5:30]] — "I can of mine own self do nothing"; cited by Blavatsky beside Buddhist sayings on the Path. The word here is an ordinary reflexive.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/40 Matthew/Matthew 006#^kjv-mat-6-6|Matthew 6:6]] — prayer in the closet. Blavatsky cites the verse elsewhere, and in footnote 1108 ties the same counsel to the Vedantic inner self without printing the reference.
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/43 John/John 014#^kjv-jhn-14-6|John 14:6]] — quoted by Hall on the page where he equates salvation with finding the "true Self".
- [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/47 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 004#^kjv-2co-4-16|2 Corinthians 4:16]] and [[Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/48 Galatians/Galatians 005#^kjv-gal-5-17|Galatians 5:17]] — outward and inward man; Spirit against flesh. Editorial match only: the Wilberforce sermon quoted in SELF, LOWER echoes both ("your outward man is perishing") with no reference printed.

## Hall — *The Secret Teachings of All Ages*

- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Mystic Christianity#^hall-stota-pdf-593|PDF 593]] — "Union with the immortal Self constitutes immortality, and he who finds his true Self is therefore 'saved.'" Hall identifies this Self with the Christos.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Stones, Metals and Gems#^hall-stota-pdf-304|PDF 304]] — the wedding ring witnesses "the union of the Higher Self (God) with the lower self (Nature)", the hermetic marriage.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Atlantis and the Godsof Antiquity#^hall-stota-pdf-92|PDF 92]] — the Cabiric brother slain by his brethren is "the allegory of the Self murdered by the not-self".
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Ancient Mysteries and Secret Societies Part Three#^hall-stota-pdf-71|PDF 71]] — on the Eleusinian teaching: whoever takes the body for "his real self" loses the chance to unfold "his immortal, invisible Self".
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Conclusion#^hall-stota-pdf-672|PDF 672]] — philosophy shows man "a diviner Self, through which he is one with the symphony of the Whole".

## Bullinger — *The Witness of the Stars*

Nothing relevant: the dossier finds no use of the word in the book.

## Blavatsky — *Isis Unveiled*

- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/11 Chapter VII Early Christian Heresies and Secret Societies#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_342|Vol. 2, page 342]] — her own voice: the Eclectic school and the Yogin share "the communion of the Brahman with his own luminous Self—the 'Atman'". She quotes "Self is the Lord of all things" as from the Rig-Veda and sets the Idra Rabba and the Sohar beside it.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/11 Chapter VII Early Christian Heresies and Secret Societies#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_317|Vol. 2, page 317]] with [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/21 Footnotes#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Footnote_661|footnote 661]] — the first adept "felt the great Being within himself", the Âtman. The footnote is a long quotation from Max Müller on how Âtman "comes to mean Self"; there she is reporting Müller.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/06 Chapter II Christian Crimes and Heathen Virtues#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_115|Vol. 2, page 115]] — her own account of initiation: the candidate is allowed "to behold his future self", which she equates with the Greek Augoeides.
- [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/18 Chapter XI Comparative Results of Buddhism and Christianity#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Page_565|Vol. 2, page 565]], [[Books/Isis Unveiled (H. P. Blavatsky)/Volume 2 Theology/Text/21 Footnotes#^blavatsky-isis-v2-Footnote_1108|footnote 1108]] — her own gloss: Jesus' closet prayer is the Vedantin's retirement to the "inner self".

## Cross-source comparison (editorial)

| Relation | Supported observation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Gaskell and Hall | Both write "Higher Self" and "lower self" and both equate the Higher Self with Christ or Christos | Shared vocabulary of the period. Hall does not cite Gaskell |
| Gaskell and Blavatsky | Both ground the capital-S Self in Indian texts (Gita; Veda and Upanishad) | Gaskell maps it onto a fixed scheme of planes; Blavatsky argues historical identity of schools. No mutual citation is claimed from the pages read |
| Hall and Blavatsky | Both make sight of, or union with, the Self the goal of initiation | Hall's examples are Cabiric and Eleusinian; Blavatsky's are Indian and Neoplatonic |
| Bible and Bullinger | Neither uses "Self" as a term | John 5:30 and similar verses cannot be cited as biblical support for a Higher Self doctrine; that reading belongs to the interpreters |
