# Sacrifice

## Working definition (editorial scaffolding)

Giving something up, or giving oneself up, to a higher claim. Gaskell and Hall turn the altar inward: what is offered is the lower nature. Bullinger keeps it outward and historical: one atoning death, pictured in the stars. Blavatsky treats blood atonement as an old and widespread belief that the Church inherited, and she argues against it.

## Gaskell (OCR; unreviewed)

There is no plain SACRIFICE headword in the import. The main entries are [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/S/SACRIFICE OF ANIMALS|SACRIFICE OF ANIMALS]] ([[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_650|page_650]]–[[Supporting Material/Gaskell/Source Text/page_651|page_651]]), [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/O/OFFERING, OR SACRIFICE|OFFERING, OR SACRIFICE]] and [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/S/SACRIFICER, THE|SACRIFICER, THE]].

Short definitions (OCR exact; the first is cut off by the machine selection, and the body continues "qualities of goodness, love, and truth"):

> Symbolic of the devitalising and offering up of the lower desires and affections for the sake of making a place in the soul for the higher .

> A symbol of the giving up of the lower for the sake of the higher.

> A symbol of the Supreme, who in manifestation limits his own nature and conditions his activity.

SACRIFICE OF ANIMALS reads the Laws of Manu this way: the animals are lower qualities, the gods fed by sacrifice are the soul's ideals, and the rite rebuilds "Christ, the Archetypal Man" in humanity. SACRIFICER, THE moves to cosmogony: the First Logos is both sacrifice and sacrificer, limiting itself in matter during involution.

Neighbours: [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/S/SACRIFICES, BURNT|SACRIFICES, BURNT]] (a purifying buddhic process), [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/R/RAM OF SACRIFICE|RAM OF SACRIFICE]] (the Second Logos; quotes Genesis 22:13), [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/B/BEASTS AND BIRDS SACRIFICED|BEASTS AND BIRDS SACRIFICED]].

Damaged headwords: [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/S/SACRIFICE, BEASTS, CLEAN|SACRIFICE, BEASTS, CLEAN]] sits on page_102 among the B entries and is plainly a clean-beasts entry with a stray leading word. [[Reference/Gaskell/Entries/S/SCARABAZUS SCAPEGOAT|SCARABAZUS SCAPEGOAT]] is the scapegoat entry (Leviticus 16) with another headword fused on. `SACRIFIOR CARAPACE OF THE TORTOISE` is a tortoise-shell entry and not about sacrifice.

## Hall — *The Secret Teachings of All Ages*

- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Tabernacle in the Wilderness#^hall-stota-pdf-415|PDF 415]] — at the Altar of Burnt Offerings the candidate must offer not a bull or ram "but its correspondence within his own nature". Hall adds that pagan animal and human sacrifice came from ignorance of this principle.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Ancient Mysteries and Secret Societies#^hall-stota-pdf-45|PDF 45]] — the Mysteries taught "a worthy life as the most acceptable sacrifice to God".
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Life and Philosophy of Pythagoras#^hall-stota-pdf-199|PDF 199]] — the Pythagorean aphorism "Nourish a cock, but sacrifice it not", read first as a warning against sacrificing living things.
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/Atlantis and the Godsof Antiquity#^hall-stota-pdf-92|PDF 92]] — Odin hanging on the World Tree: "Wisdom, not death, is the reward for this voluntary sacrifice".
- [[Books/The Secret Teachings of All Ages (Manly P. Hall)/Text/The Cross and the Crucifixion#^hall-stota-pdf-611|PDF 611]] — Hall quotes J. P. Lundy asking whether a Hindu plate shows the Victim-Man "who offered himself a sacrifice before the worlds were". The question is Lundy's.

## Bullinger — *The Witness of the Stars*

- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/10 Chapter I The Sign CAPRICORNUS#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00022|Capricornus]] — his Second Book opens with the Goat and closes with the Ram, "two animals of sacrifice"; the Goat is the Atoning Sacrifice, the fish-tail the people atoned for.
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/06 Chapter II The Sign LIBRA#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00011|Crux]] and [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/06 Chapter II The Sign LIBRA#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00012|the Victim]] — the Southern Cross faded from Jerusalem's sky "when the Real Sacrifice was offered there"; the Victim is slain by the Centaur, that is "by Himself".
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/11 Chapter II The Sign AQUARIUS#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00026|Aquarius]] — from Abel's lamb onward, "atonement made is the only foundation of blessing."
- [[Books/The Witness of the Stars (E. W. Bullinger)/Text/21 Appendix Note on the Sign LIBRA#^bullinger-wots-pgepubid00057|Appendix on Libra]] — Libra's scales replaced an older Altar, an enemy attempt to "substitute human merit for the atoning sacrifice of Christ".

## Cross-source comparison (editorial)

| Relation | Supported observation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial resemblance | Gaskell and Hall both make the animal on the altar a figure for the offerer's own lower nature | Hall does not cite Gaskell; Gaskell's planes vocabulary (buddhic, astral) is absent from Hall |
| Direct opposition | Bullinger's appendix condemns replacing atonement with human merit; the inward readings of Gaskell and Hall are what he would call that | He names neither; the opposition is editorial |
| Shared material, opposite use | Bullinger and Blavatsky both note sacrifice across ancient nations | For him it is corrupted primitive truth; for her it shows the doctrine is pagan in origin |
| Self-offering before creation | Gaskell's SACRIFICER and Lundy (in Hall) both mention a divine victim who offers himself before the worlds | Gaskell asserts it; Hall only relays Lundy's question |
