## Argument of the chapter (as she makes it)

Prejudice and “public opinion,” she says, magnetically enclose people; to become a “genuine spiritual entity” a person must recreate himself by eliminating selfishness, superstition, and that infection ([[07 Chapter II Phenomena and Forces#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_39|pages 39–40]]). Science has inherited theology’s claim of infallibility. Modern spiritualism, after fraud is discounted, still presents genuine mysterious phenomena that scientists will not examine in good faith; she calls the resulting panic *scientific psychophobia* (pages 40–47). Trivial or illiterate “communications” from famous names disgust investigators, but that surface scum does not, for her, disprove a deeper truth.

She rests her right to speak on “many years’ study of both ancient magic and its modern form, Spiritualism,” and on travel among fakirs, dervishes, marabouts, and serpent-charmers (pages 42–43). Those travel claims are her narrative, not independently verified here.

William Crookes and the Dialectical Society are her chief modern witnesses that unexplained manifestations occur, without their being able to name the agency (pages 44–46). She reprints Crookes’s eight theories (page 47). Fraud, delusion, and “cerebral action” she sets aside as insufficient for the remainder. The interesting options are: the medium’s own spirit; evil spirits (the theologians’ theory); “a separate order of beings” — demons not necessarily bad, “gnomes, fairies, kobolds, elves, goblins, Puck” — “one of the claims of the kabalists”; departed human beings; and “psychic force” as an adjunct. Katie King, in Crookes’s house, she will not take as a departed mortal; she presses Crookes’s sixth (elemental) theory (pages 48–49). Scientists, she says, must study the “triple nature of man—physiological, psychological, and *divine*” as the old magicians did (page 49).

Lost arts (malleable glass, the *alkahest*, a correspondent’s “true oil of gold”) are offered as evidence that ancient and hermetic chemistry outran the moderns (pages 50–51). Footnote 122 adds that Paracelsus secretly rediscovered hydrogen and that Rosicrucian alchemists knew oxygen — her chemical-historical claims. Cycles of races return (page 51). Much current mediumship is fraud, but some communications are philosophically impressive; she warns that undiscriminating spiritualists, harassed by “unprincipled and lying ‘spirits’,” may within twenty-five years “fly to the Romish communion” unless they study ancient philosophy (pages 53–54). That forecast is hers; it is not a reading of unread Volume 2 chapters.

Henry More is quoted: many necromantic apparitions are “*ludicrous spirits, not the souls of the deceased*,” yet More himself is “clear for the appearing of the soul of Samuel,” while allowing that a shape-changing spirit “might *personate* Samuel” (pages 54–55). She prints no chapter number. The installed narrative of that episode is [[1 Samuel 028#^kjv-1sa-28-13|1 Samuel 28]] (the woman of Endor). That is an editorial attachment of the discussed story, not her numbered citation. More’s own dual possibility (real Samuel *or* a personating spirit) is not collapsed into one doctrine.

The many scientific names for the unknown agency (psychic force, psychode/ectenic force, electro-biological power, unconscious cerebration, ideo-motor principle) “can be condensed in two words: the kabalistic ASTRAL LIGHT” ([[07 Chapter II Phenomena and Forces#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_58|page 58]]). She aligns Schopenhauer’s *will*, Plato’s Demiurgic *Nous* and dodecahedron, the Chaldean Oracles, Philo, Orphic Eros-Phanes, and the Hindu Maha-Atma with the theurgists’ Universal Soul / *Anima Mundi* / Astral Light (pages 55–57). The Cosmos is “the Son” with Plato; an older Horus remains as Idea in the Demiurgic Mind, a younger Horus is that Idea “going forth from the *Logos*” and clothed with matter (page 56). Those identifications are hers (via Dunlap, Movers, Plutarch).

Will, following Van Helmont and Paracelsus, is “the first of all powers.” Unbelief can neutralize a medium: she quotes, without a printed reference, “And he did not many mighty works there, because of their unbelief” (page 57). That wording matches [[Matthew 013#^kjv-mat-13-58|Matthew 13:58]]. Orphic “Wind” as “the spirit of God… ‘brooding over the Chaos’” (page 56) uses biblical phrasing neighbouring [Genesis 1:2](<Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/01 Genesis/Genesis 001.md#^kjv-gen-1-2>) but she does not cite the verse; treat that as unnumbered resemblance, not an explicit citation.

“As God creates, so man can create”: sufficiently intense will makes a mental form first subjective, then “concrete, visible, objective”; “the man has learned the secret of secrets; he is a MAGICIAN” (pages 61–62). Faraday’s table tests, Babinet’s muscular-vibration theory, and Agassiz’s refusal are dismissed as missing an exterior or interior *will* (pages 60–64).

The later pages state her discrimination of agencies more sharply than the glossary did:

- Not all communicating intelligences are Elemental or Elementary; many who control a medium *subjectively* are “human, disembodied spirits,” their character depending on the medium, the circle, and the purpose (page 67).
- “Human spirits can *never* materialize themselves *in propria personâ*.” At most they project an “æthereal reflection”; a warm, solid, sweating form, however like a known person, is not that person’s body but a “portrait statue” constructed by elementaries (pages 67–70). She says she has herself seen such recognized figures and still denies they were the persons.
- A respectable *umbra* is “visible but impalpable,” “luminous and vapory” (page 69), continuing the threefold *manes / anima / umbra* vocabulary of Chapter I.
- Spectral animals that talk, incite crime, or behave unlike living beasts are elementals (or else one must admit surviving animal souls); she prefers Porphyry’s intermediary demons (pages 70–71).

The chapter closes with Paracelsus as rediscoverer of the magnet (“the bone of Horus”) and Mesmer as the later disciple who took the public credit (pages 71–72).

## What this vault should not do with Chapter II

- Do not treat Katie King, the alkahest, or her travel among fakirs as verified events.
- Do not equate Crookes’s “psychic force” with Gaskell’s LIGHT, or her denial of full human materialization with Hall’s later elemental lore, beyond the documented editorial resemblance of the fourfold/goblin list.
- Do not read the Rome-warning as a summary of unread Volume 2.
- Do not attach Genesis 1:2 as if she numbered it.
