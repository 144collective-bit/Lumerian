## Argument (as she makes it)

Genuine physical phenomena, she repeats, are seldom caused by disembodied human spirits, but even occult forces used by Indian and Egyptian “jugglers” deserve scientific study ([[08 Chapter III Blind Leaders of the Blind#^blavatsky-isis-v1-Page_73|pages 73–74]]). She challenges Western prestidigitateurs to match three feats she says were shown to members of the Prince of Wales’s suite: a clasped rupee becoming a cobra; a chosen seed fruiting in a quarter of an hour; suspension on three sword-points, then on air. Those reports are hers (or her informants’), not independently verified here.

She quotes Elihu, “great men are not always wise; neither do the aged understand judgment” (page 74; footnote 150: “Job”). That wording matches [[Job 032#^kjv-job-32-9|Job 32:9]]. Henry More is cited again on biblical histories assuring men of angels and spirits.

The long middle of the chapter attacks American Positivists as “irascible psychophobists” who would uproot faith in God and immortality by ridiculing Spiritualism (pages 75–83). Comte is treated as a “prophet” of a godless religion; she attacks him as a reformer, not as a mathematician. She finds his “woman of the future” and “artificial fecundation” more preposterous than theological or spiritualist “vagaries,” and quotes Huxley calling Comte’s practice “*Catholicism minus Christianity*” (pages 82–83). Professor Hare is quoted: Positive philosophy is “merely negative.” Spiritualism, she says, has never pretended to be more than a science or a research into unexplained forces (page 83).

Scientists in general are then charged with conservative dogmatism: they first mock discoveries, then appropriate them (pages 84–86). She protests Draper’s habit of laying all resistance to science at the clergy’s door and leaving scientists’ own opposition unnamed. In a figure drawn from Genesis without a printed reference, science now “seize[s] the sceptre of despotism… like the cherubim and flaming sword of Eden, to keep the people away from the tree of immortal life” (page 85). That is rhetorical use of the Eden image, not a verse citation; see the review queue rather than the Tree of Life hub as if she were expositing Genesis 3.

Tyndall’s “impassable chasm” between brain-physics and consciousness is answered by pointing to Buchanan’s anthropology and to “spiritual intuitions” (pages 86–87). On testimony she cites Moses: “At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death” (page 87; footnote 170: Deuteronomy xvii. 6), matching [[Deuteronomy 017#^kjv-deu-17-6|Deuteronomy 17:6]]. Scientists, she says, will not accept the evidence of millions.

Medicine is singled out as especially prejudiced (pages 88–89). A modern defender (C. B. Warring, fn. 172) who “seriously endeavors to identify” Peruvian bark with the paradisiacal Tree of Life is reported, not adopted.

The last third turns East. Hindu gymnosophists are said to have held magic as divine, more esoteric than even Egyptian priests (pages 89–90). Footnote 174 (Haug, as she uses him) places the seven Rishis in a position “answering to that of the twelve sons of Jacob”; that is her/Haug’s comparison, not a numbered verse citation. Biblical miracles are accepted facts for Christians, while *Atharva-Veda* wonders are treated as diabolism; she infers that if one borrowed, it was not the older Vedas that plagiarized (pages 90–91).

Brahma, she insists, is a *secondary* creating god, “like Jehovah… ‘a *mover of the waters*’” (page 91). In allegorical pictures he has four heads answering to the four cardinal points and is the *demiurgos*. Polier’s cosmogony (Brahma on a lotus, floating on waters, addressing Parabrahma) is set beside Egyptian Athtor/Mother Night and “the Jewish Scriptures,” though footnote 177 warns that she means “the *real* Jewish one explained kabalistically,” not “the current or accepted Bible.” Brahma as *Narayana* “moves on the eternal waters, with the spirit of God within himself.”

The lotus, shared by Hindus, Egyptians, and (as lilies in the Annunciation) Judaeo-Christian art, is “the emblem of the productive power of nature, through the agency of fire and water (spirit and matter)” and later “the product of fire (heat) and water” (pages 91–93). Wherever used, it signifies “the emanation of the objective from the concealed.” Lotus seeds already containing miniature leaves become, for her, “the origin and explanation” of the verse “And God said, Let the earth bring forth … the fruit-tree yielding fruit after his kind, *whose seed is in itself*” — installed [Genesis 1:11](<Books/King James Bible (Project Gutenberg 10)/01 Genesis/Genesis 001.md#^kjv-gen-1-11>) (and 1:12). In primitive religions “the ‘Son of the Father’ is the creative God”; Brahma, Jehovah (Adam-Kadmon), and Osiris or rather Pimander occupy the second place of a trinity; Pimander is “the root of all the Egyptian Sun-gods”; “the Eternal is the Spirit of Fire” that fructifies what is born of water. The lily of Gabriel is “worked into the earliest dogma of the baptismal sacrament” (page 93). Those identifications are hers.

Bruno and Spinoza seek God “within nature and not without.” Against Draper, she argues from Berti’s documents that Bruno was a Pythagorean who believed in survival (metempsychosis as his accuser described it), held Jesus a magician in Porphyry’s sense of *divina sapientia*, and confessed an infinite universe of worlds with a double Providence (pages 93–98). Bruno, as she quotes him, understood the Holy Spirit “according to the manner shown by Solomon” as the soul of the universe: “The spirit of God filled all the earth, and that which contains all things” (page 97). That is Bruno quoting “the wisdom of Solomon”; the vault’s installed KJV tree has no Wisdom of Solomon book, so the quotation is not given a verse link here. He also cites *Ecclesiastes*: “there is nothing new under the sun; that which is is that which was,” a paraphrase of [[Ecclesiastes 001#^kjv-ecc-1-9|Ecclesiastes 1:9]]. The chapter ends: fragments of modern philosophy were “dug from the Oriental mines.”

## Limits

Do not treat the Indian juggler reports, Comte-psychopathology, or Bruno’s inner faith as independently verified. Do not merge her kabalistic Jehovah with the installed Genesis wording, nor her lotus-baptism typology with unread Volume 2.
