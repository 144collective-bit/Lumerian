MYSTERY MYRTLE, BURNT :— A symbol of envy. MYSIA REGION :— A symbol of the stage of pure intellect. MYSTERIES OF LIGHT :— A symbol of the higher nature suffused in the light of Truth, which is a mystery to the lower mind. It refers to the raising of the consciousness to a higher level, and the arousing of latent powers in the soul. See Liout, Mrpst. | MYSTERIES AND MAGIC OF THE THIRTEENTH ZON :~ Symbolic of the awakening of powers which have hitherto lain dormant, until the cyclic period which is indicated by this fon of final liberation from the lower conditions. See ALON (thirteen). MYSTERY, FIRST :— A symbol of purity, gentleness, and obedience to the calls of duty and love. *‘The disciples thought that all had been revealed (by Jesus), and that the First Mystery—the Father in the likeness of a dove,—was the gnosis of all gnoses.”’ —Pistis Sophia. The disciplined qualities mistakenly thought that revelation and finality are synonymous. This is an error into which many more or leas enlightened people fall. The First Mystery as a dove is an emblem of simplicity, meekness, and willingness to descend to everyday duties of life. “The way to the eternal glory lies through a right relationship to the common things of life, not in despising and forsaking them. The man of faith is he who discerns the spirituality inherent in material existence, and _ lives accordingly. And to him who so lives God reveals the truth that everything earthly is more and other than it seems ; is, in fact, divine.’—R. J. CampsELt, Serm., Steadying Power of Faith. See Dove, Gnosis, Mrpst. MYSTERY-NAME :— A symbol which conveys no special signification to the lower mind, but one which indicates the idea signified to the individuality or higher mind. See ABERAMENTHO. or to MYTHOLOGY MYTHOLOGY :— This contains the symbolism of Divine emanation from the Absolute, and manifestation of Spirit and Matter in Time and Space throughout the cycle of Life: the symbolism of the origin and growth of the Soul, the descent of Spirit into Matter in the process of Involution, followed by the ascent of Spirit from Matter in the process of Evolution: the symbolism of the five planes of being, and of the higher and lower natures, and of the four soul- bodies : the symbolism of the struggle in the soul between the Divine principle or Self and the lower principle or not-Self; and also of a great variety of unapparent activities. ‘‘And yet, how strange (are these tales)! From the very childhood of philosophy, from the first faintly whispered y? to our own time of matured thought and fearless inquiry, mythology has been the ever-recurrent subject of anxious wonder and careful study. . . . There is this common feature in all who have thought or written on mythology, that they look upon it as something which, whatever it may mean, does certainly not mean what it seems to mean; as something that requires an explanation, whether it bo a system of religion, or a phase in the development of the human mind.’’—Max MULLER, Chips, Vol. IV. pp. 156-7. “‘It may pass for a further indication of a concealed and secret meaning, that some of these (Greek) fables are so absurd, and idle, in their narration, as to show and proclaim an allegory even afar off. A fable that carries probability with it may be supposed to be invented for leasure, or in imitation of history; ut those fables that could never be conceived, or related in this way, must surely have a different use. For example, what a monstrous fiction is this, that Jupiter should take Metis to wife; and as soon as he found her pregnant, eat her up; whereby he also conceived, and out of his head brought forth Pallas armed ? Certainly no mortal could, but for the sake of the moral it couches, invent such an absurd dream: so much out of the road of thought.’’—Lorp Bacon, Wisdom of the Ancients. ‘* Let the narrations in the Eddas be compared with the beginning of Hesiod’s Theogony, with the mythology of some Asiatic nations, and with the book of Genesis, and we shall instantly be convinced that the conformity which is found between many circumstances of their recitals cannot be the mere work of chance.’’—Matuet, North.
