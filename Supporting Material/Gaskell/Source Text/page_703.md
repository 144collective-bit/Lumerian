SKANDHAS SKADE :— A symbol of Divine Law. SKAITYA FLY, DEATH-DEALING :— A symbol of the critical intellect which dispels error and illusion. SKANDHAS, FIVE :— Symbols of the five elemental bodies or vestures of the soul, as instruments for the manifestation of spirit. “Every being is com of five constituent elements called Skandhas, which have their source in Upddana, and are continually combining, dissolving, and recombining, viz. (1) Form (rupa), i.e. the organised body. (2) Sensation (wedand) of pain or pleasure, or of neither, arising from contact of eye, ear, ete. with external objects. (3) Perception (sanna) of ideas through tho same sixfold contact. (14) Aggregate of formations (samkhGra), i.e. combination of properties or faculties or mental tendencies, fiftytwo in number, forming individual character and derived from previous existences, (5) Consciousness (tinndna) or thought. This fifth is the most important. It is the only soul recognised by Buddhists. Theoretically it perishes with the other Skandhas, but practically is continued, since its exact counterpart is reproduced in a new body.”—Mon. WILLIAMS, Buddhiem, p. 109. The bodies are taken in_ their receptive aspects towards the energies upon the several planes. Placing them in their proper order, the five bodies are,— Samkhéra = Buddhic ; Vinnéina = Causal; Sanna = Mental ; Vedan&é = Astral; Ripa = Physical. The Samkhdra is described as the potential higher causal-body containing the prototypes of all things and qualities that have to be evolved on the planes below. These prototypes are derived from the experience of previous life-cycles. They are perfect and complete (5 + 2 = 7), and guide the formation of the character of the units in the phenomenal world. The Vinndna represents the causal-body on the higher mental plane, the seat of the higher consciousness of humanity. The causal-body persiste throughout the cycle, and periodically reproduces the transitory lower bodies 696 SKINS at each incarnation. The Senna is the mental body which gives perception of ideas formed through vibretions from the lower activities. The Vedand is the astral body of the desires, feelings, and lower emotions which are aroused by the senses. The Ripa is the physical body by means of which action is effected. SKIN OF HUMAN BODY :— A symbol of the outer envelope of the soul, which is the means of functioning upon the astral or physical plane. ‘ The interior man ia ‘ clothed with akin and flesh’ (Jos x. 9), since wherein it is raised up to things above, it is straightly blockaded with the besieging of fleshly motions. . . . ‘And hast fenced me with bones and sinews.’ With flesh and skin we are clothed, but we are ‘fenced with bones and sinews,’ in that though we receive a shock by temptation assaulting us from without, yet the hand of the Creator strengthens us within, that we should not be shattered. And so by the promptings of the flesh, He abases us in respect of His gifts, but by the bones of virtue He strengthens us against temptations.”—-St. Greaory, Morals on the Book of Job, Vol. I. p. 552.
