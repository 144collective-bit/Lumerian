A symbol of the Divine Ray, the Sutratma, or Tree of Life. ‘* The kusht: consists of 72 fibres woven into twelve strands of 6 fibres each, the 12 strands being further woven into 3 cords of 4 strands each. These 3 cords, which are plaited together to form the kushtt, represent the three fundamental principles of the Zoroastrian faith, good thoughts, good words, and good deeds; the other subdivisions having each in like manner a symbolical meaning.’"—E. G. Browne, A Year among the Persians, p. 369. Sir Monier Williams informs us that,— ‘‘the girdle must then be coiled round the body three times and fastened with two particular knots (said to represent the sun and moon), which none but a Parsee can tie in a proper manner.”’— Modern India, p. 96. The two knots are the symbols of the Higher Self (sun) and the lower self (moon); the first in the causalbody, and the second in the mental body, both being centres upon the Divine Ray from above. The twelve strands represent human experience in the twelve divisions of the cycle of life, leading to soulgrowth. The three cords made from the strands of experience, symbolise the law of growth upon the four lower planes. The Divine law insists upon the regulation of the life by the soul itself, which shows that there is no abstract, or vicarious, goodness to rely upon, and that apart from Thought, Speech, and Action, duly regulated, there is no salvation for the soul. The 72 fibres relate to 72 KUVERA potential and actual conditions of experience on the physical, astral and mental planes. See BARHIS, BARSOM, Brrr (second) BREASTPLATE, BUCKLE, EpHOD, TAMAGUSHI, Woop, YAGNOPAVITA.
