A symbol of the soul. ‘* But before Tangaroa fled, his children consulted together how they might secure their safety, for Tangaroa had begotten Punga, and he had begotten two children, Ika-tere, the father of fish, and Tute-wehiwehi, the father of reptiles.” —G. GREY, Polynesian Mythology. But before the obscuration of Spirit, the Logos (Tangaroa) had brought the soul (Punga) into active being. The soul was endowed with two capacities for development ; one springing from above,—the capacity (Ika-tere) for aspiration (fish), and the other rising by reflection from below, the capacity for desire (reptiles). These capacities became operants in the evolution of the soul. See DotpHiIn, FisH, IKA-TERE, Jxsus (fish), Ranoei, Sout (middle), TANGAROA, TUTE.
