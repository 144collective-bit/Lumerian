A symbol of primordial Matter as emanated from the Absolute. This Divine essence differentiates into seven planes of manifestation, two of which,—the highest,—are potential, and five are actual. Spirit working into and through Matter makes matter fertile for the production of all forms and qualities on the four lower planes. ‘‘The source of all things is the power of the divine spirit (Maya), the power that is hidden beneath the things that emanate out of it. It is the one deity that actuates and controls all those roposed principles of emanation, include fe time and the personal soul.” —Svetas. Upanishad, §1. &. B. of £. PRALAYA . “* Maya, or Prakriti, a birthlees beginning that gives birth to all things... . Prakriti is May&é, and Mahesvara is the Mayin or arch-illusionist. All this shifti world is filled with portions of him.”’— Ibtd. ‘*The root and substance of all thi (except soul, Purusha) is Prakriti. It is no production. Seven things produced by it are also producers. Thence come sixteen productions. Soul, the twentyfifth essence, is neither a production nor roducer.”’ — Mon. Wriiams, Indian Wisdom, p. 92. Much preparation is needed before the Self or Soul can enter the Worldprocess. States of being produce other states following, and many states are begun and ended before the lower nature is in a condition capable of bearing the Divine Child,—the God within. The Self is not a product, but is the eternal Spirit, and comes to be the ruler and organiser of the entire nature. ‘* By various means Prakriti endowed with qualities, acting as a benefactress, accomplishes without profit to herself the purpose of soul, who is devoid of ualities, and makes no return of benet.”—San-khya-karika, 59, 60. The soul being essentially Spirit, — eternal, unchangeable,—is regarded as ** witness, solitary, bystander, spectator, and passive” (tbid., 19), before whom the qualities of matter (prakriti) are displayed. ‘** Prakriti, the principle of emanation, unconscious as it is, acts with a view to the liberation of Purushas or Selves. . . - Prior to a fresh creation, or palingenesia of the world, there is no misery, as the migrating souls have neither bodies, senses, nor environments.’’— A. E. Gouan, Phil. of Upanishads, p. 205. *‘The Samkhya philosopher makes Prakriti, under the eye of a Purusha (Self), develop into Buddhi.”—Max MOLLER, The Six Systems, p. 371. See AKASA, BINAH, BUDDHI, CHARIOT, COW-MEHURT, EVOLUTION, HETHRA, Itiusron, INVOLUTION, Matrer, MAyA, Morner, MULA-PRAKRITI, PLANES, PurvusHA, SELF, Sprrit, WoMB.
