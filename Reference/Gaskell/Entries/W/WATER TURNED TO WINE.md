A symbol of the substitution of the divine life or love element for 803 WATERPOTS the duty element in the conduct of life. It is a transmutation of the lower mental into the higher spiritual condition of soul. “The ‘ beginning of miracles’ (JOHN i. 11) for the Man Regenerate is always the transmutation of the ‘ water’ of his own Soul into the ‘ Wine’ of the Divine Spirit.”"—The Perfect Way, p. 229. ** But by the Divine pore the water is changed into wine when perfect love casts out fear.”"—Sr. BeRNanp, Serm. I, tn Dom. I, post. Oct. Ep. “By these nuptials and by wine, He signified the union, and as it were, marriage of our soul, through grace and charity, with God.""—C. & Lappe, Great Comm. St. John, p. 90. “The miracle apart, there lies something mysterious and sacramental in the very fact. Let us knock, that He may open to us, and fill us with the invisible wine: for we were water, and he made us wine, made us wise; for He gave us the wisdom of His faith, whilst before we were foolish.”—AvuausTINE, Gospel of John, Vol. I, p. 1165. **When the author of this (fourth) gospel asked himself what Jesus had done or them, he conceives of His work as the transmutation of the common ordinary life into a thing of spiritual value and religious reality, that is, He made water into wine. If this be the meaning of the story of the miracle at Cana, it is much more valuable than if we took it in a literal sense. To believe that Jesus on one particular occasion turned literal water into wine would not be a religious belief at all, nor of any value for religion now. But to believe that He, by the spiritual quality of His mind, showed the deeper meaning, and brought out the divineness, of the common ordinary life, is to recognise a truth of daily importance for ourselves.’""—T. Roonppa WiLLiIaMs, Serm., Miracle of Cana. See Cana, DISPENSATIONS, SACRAMENT, TRANSMUTATION, WINE.
