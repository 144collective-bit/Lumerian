A symbol of the Higher Self or Divine Ego. **A son was born to Vivanh&ao, Yima. khshaéta, who had abundance of flocks, YOMI The invitation of the Spirit is to those who are oppressed with the cares of life, and full of worldly aims. The Christ pointe out the advantage of resting the mind upon the ideal which is above the lower ambitions and _ affections. The ‘taking of the yoke, etc.,” signifies the joyful acceptance of the ideals of righteousness and truth—burdens, as the lower nature would regard them —which has the moral effect of transforming life with its environment into a joyous playground rather than @ penitentiary. “For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.”’— 1 Joun v. 3. ***To Buddha will I look in faith: he, the Exalted, is the holy, supreme Buddha, the knowing, the instructed, the blessed, who knows the worlds, the Supreme One, who yoketh men like an ox, the Teacher of gods and men, the Exalted Buddha.’"—H. OLpEensrne, Buddha, p. 339. The Self within (Buddha) draws the mental qualities (men) and the ideals (gods) to himself by the bonds of love (yoke). **For what heavy yoke does He put upon our mind’s neck, who bids us shun every desire that causes disquietude ? What heavy burden does He lay upon His followers, who warns us to decline the wearisome ways of the world ?” **The Lord beheld this heavy yoke of slavery set hard on the necks of worldly men—a weight of heavy bondage to be subject to temporal concerns, to court the things of earth, to retain things which are gliding away, to wish to stand in things which stand not, to seek after passing objects, but yet be unwilling to pass away with what is passing. .. . But the bands of each one are loosed, when by Divine help the inward bonds of carnal desire are burst asunder.’’—Sr. GREGORY THE GREAT, Morals on_ the Book of Job, Vol. I. p. 132; Vol. ITI. p- 399. *** This restful labouring,’ said Walter Hilton, ‘is full far from fleshly idleness and from blind security. It is full of spiritual working, but it is called rest, for that grace looseth the heavy yoke of fleshly love from the soul and maketh it mighty and free through the gift of spiritual love for to work gladly, softly, and delectably.’ ""—E. UNDERHILL, Mysttctem, p. 60. See Heart, JxEsus, LABOURERS, LAW OF ZOROASTER, POOR IN SPIRIT, Rico MAN, RioHEs (lower), SERMON, Worx, 837
