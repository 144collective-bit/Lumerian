```text
A symbol of the personality, or the lower self in the mental body. OF DUR-YODHANA ** Inasmuch as he lays down the dviyagus brick close to the durva-brick (cattle), —the durva-brick being the breath and the dviyagus the Sacrificer,—those two bodies of his (human and divine) thus become connected by _breath.’’—~Sata. Br&h., VII. 4, 2, 20. The two centres of consciousness,— the Individuality and the Personality, —are interconnected upon the mental plane. The human personality is the expression on the lower planes of the Divine individuality. See AtTaR (fire), BREATH, BRICKS, Dvryacus, PERSONALITY, SACRIFICER. DUR-YODHANA, ‘‘ DIFFICULT TO BE SUBDUED ”’ :— A symbol of the desire-mind, the principle of kama-manas. “‘Dur-yodhana (like Ravana) is a visible type of the evil principle in human nature for ever doing battle with the good and divine principle, symbolised by the five sons of Pandu.’’—Mon. Witiiams, Indian Wisdom, p. 383. The ‘hundred brothers”? of Duryodhana are the desires. See ADVERSARY, DEVIL, Kurvus, PANDAVAS, RAVANA. Durira, DUSAHK ABYSS ~— A symbol of the lower planes, the arena of the desire-mental activities ; the lower life of suffering and discipline in which the incarnating egos find themselves. See BrincE (kinvat), GARODMAN, Haves, HELL, PURGATORY.
```
