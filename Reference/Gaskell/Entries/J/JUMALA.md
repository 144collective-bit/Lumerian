A symbol of the Divine Will or Higher Self, Atma-buddhi. ‘*Jumala meant originally the sky. It is derived, as Castrén has wn (Pp. 24), from Juma, thunder, and la, the place, meaning therefore the place of thunder, or the sky. It is used first of all for sky, secondly for the god of the sky, and thirdly for gods in general.” —Max MULLER, Science of Religion, p. 133. ‘*God, the supreme creator—omnipotent Jumala.”’—Kalevala.
