Symbolic of the commencement and termination of the cycle of life through which the Self passes, as comparable to the sun in its diurnal course from east to west. ** Heru-khuti, ic. Horus of the two horizons, is a form of the Sun-god ; the words ‘ two horizons’ refer to the mountains of Bakhatet and Manu, the most easterly and westerly points of the sun’s course, and the places where he rose and set.”’-BupGeE, Book of the Dead, p. 4. “I know the two sycamore trees of turquoise, from between which the god R& doth emerge when he setteth out upon his journey over the pillars of Shu towards the door of the lord of the East wherefrom R& cometh forth.”— Ibid., Ch. CXLIX. The ‘“trees™ or ‘mountains ™ signify the two highest planes, atmabuddhi, and the “ pillars" denote the quaternary of planes below the plane of atma. The journey over the quaternary typifies the lower. existence in which the Self (sun) is unapperent until he rises in the “East " in the great day of the Lord. See Ease, Evtsau, Gate (tchesert), Horus, LIon-cop, MANVANTARA, PILLARS OF sHU, Ri, Sanp, Sun, SuN-BISING, SYCAMORE, WEs?, ZODIAC. HORN ON THE HEAD :-— A symbol in its higher aspect, of aspiration and lofty thought, and in its lower aspect of ambition, pride, vanity, conceit, egotism. ** Horns denote powers of truth from ..'—SwEDENBORG, Arc. Cel.,n. 10,186. **I (the Soul) make a way among the horns of all those who ee "Bok of strong against me.’’—Bupar, Book o the Dead, Ch. LXIV. ** All the horns of the wicked also will I cut off ; but the horns of the righteous shall be lifted up.’’—Ps. lxxv. 10. (See Ps. xviii. 2, and Ps. xcii. 10.) See SuHrp oF MANv, Tusk.
