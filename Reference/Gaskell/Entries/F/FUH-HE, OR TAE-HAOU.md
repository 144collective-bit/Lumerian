Symbol of the Logos. ‘*Tae-haou was also called Fuh-he or Paou-he ‘the Sacrificer.’ The tradition is that he had no father, was the first to reign, and that his name was Fung, meaning wind, spirit, breath.’’—Kupp, China, p. 103. Under this aspect of the going forth to manifest, the Logos is said to have been the ‘“‘sacrifice’’ and the ‘“‘sacrificer,’—that which offers Selflimitation to Itself, or is limited and offered up to the Absolute, for the sake of Its universe. It is mentally received that the Supreme is Selfderived, not begotten nor created, but Self-existent. The terms Word, Wind, Spirit, and Breath are all symbols of emanation,—to which the forth-pouring from the Absolute is comparable. **'You and I are here, and the whole cosmos of which we are a part exists in order that God may be able to express that which he eternally is. There are some things impossible even to omnitence, and one of them is that love's est resources should ever be able to declare themselves apart from sacrifice or that good, as good, could rightly be called such if it had never been thrown upon the dark background of evil. I do not mean to suggest that God, the eternal] reality, does not eternally know himself to be essentially goodness and love; I only say that if he is to live it, he cannot do so on any other terms than I have just stated ; it is a necessity written in the very nature of things... . Understand what you are. You are one means whereby the eternal declares itself, brings into manifestation its latent wealth of ideal good. In fact, you yourself are the eternal in one of ite manifold cts. That which is essential to your being has neither beginning nor end; it eternally is—in God. It is foolish to speak of life as though it were eternal only in one direction—the future.”’ —R. J. CamPBELL, Serm., Solidarity of Spiritual Experience.
