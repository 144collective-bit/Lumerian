A symbol of the culmination of the Higher Self in the buddhic consciousness, at the beginning or the ending of a cycle. “When Aushedar becomes thirty years old, the sun stands still in the zenith of the sky for ten days and ten nights, and it arrives again at that place where it was first appointed by allotment."’— The Dinkard, S.B. of E. When the Christ (Aushedar) has arrived at the stature of manhood, that is, when the buddhic consciousness has been attained by the soul, the Higher Self as the individuality (sun) becomes fully (ten) established, having evolved up to that point on the buddhic plane from whence it had fallen, or from whence it had proceeded in entering upon the cycle of involution. (This statement refers to development on the buddhic plane beyond the present cycle which terminates in the union of the lower Self with the Higher on the higher mental plane.) See ARREST, AUSHEDAR, I am (bull), Pisces, SUMMER, ZENITH.
