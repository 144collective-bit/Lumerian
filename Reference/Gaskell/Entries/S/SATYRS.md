A symbol of the higher emotions (buddhi-manas) united with the mind, and attached to the lower nature of the soul. The horns on the forehead and pointed ears signify lofty thought and aspiration. Their love of music and dancing indicates their self-regulating powers and disposition towards harmonious activity among themselves. “* Satyri are inseparably connected with the worship of Dionyaus, and represent the luxuriant vital powers of nature. They are always described as fond of wine, and of every kind of sensual pleasure, whence they are seen sleeping, paying musical instruments, Or engaged in vvluptuous dances with nymphs.’’—Smith’s Class. Dict. buddhiof growth through The connection of the mental qualities with the Self within (Dionysus) refers to their active relations with the Self, as development proceeds. ‘“ By man and beast together (Satyr), is signified man as to spiritual and natural affection.’"—-SWEDENBORG, Apoc. Rev., n. 567. See Dancr, Dionysus, Goris, INTOXICATION, PANS, SILENUS, WINE.
